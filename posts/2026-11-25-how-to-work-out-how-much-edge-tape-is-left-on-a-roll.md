To work out how much edge tape is left on a roll, measure the outside diameter of the roll and the core, then use the formula: length = π × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × tape thickness). All it costs is a steel rule you probably already own, it takes about a minute, and it beats eyeballing it, which is how jobs end up running short halfway through.

## Why not just eyeball it?

Because the eye lies in a predictable direction. A roll that looks "about half full" by thickness is nowhere near half its length, because each outer wrap is far longer than an inner one.

Guess high and the roll runs out mid-job, with the edgebander stopped while someone hunts the rack for the same colour. Guess low and you order a roll you didn't need.

## Method 1: measure the diameter (start here)

This is the one we'd start with. All you need is a steel rule or a set of callipers, so there's nothing to buy and you can start organising your tape today.

1. **Measure the outside diameter** of the tape across the middle of the roll.
2. **Measure the core diameter** (the outside of the cardboard or plastic core, where the tape starts).
3. **Note the tape thickness**, such as 0.4 mm, 1 mm or 2 mm.
4. **Plug it in:** length (mm) = 3.1416 × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × thickness).
5. **Divide by 1,000** to get metres.

### A worked example

Say you've got a part roll of 1 mm tape. It measures 250 mm across and the core is 80 mm.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Outer diameter squared (250²) | 62,500 |
| Core diameter squared (80²) | 6,400 |
| Difference | 56,100 |
| × 3.1416 ÷ (4 × 1 mm) | about 44,060 mm |
| **Tape left on the roll** | **about 44 m** |

Now do the same roll at 400 mm across and you get about 120 m. That's the point: the roll only got 150 mm "thicker", but it holds nearly three times the tape.

### The catch: thickness isn't always what the label says

Rolls aren't wound perfectly tight, and pre-glued tape carries a glue layer, so the real thickness per wrap is a bit bigger than the label and the formula can overestimate.

The fix is to calibrate once per tape type. Measure a new roll of known length and work the formula backwards: effective thickness = 3.1416 × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × known length in mm). Use that figure for every part roll of the same type.

## Method 2: weigh the roll

Weighing is a useful cross-check, particularly on thin tape where a millimetre of error on the diameter makes a bigger difference. The catch is you'll need an accurate scale that reads to the gram, plus a reference weight for every tape type before you can start.

1. **Weigh a full roll** of a known length.
2. **Weigh an empty core** of the same type.
3. **Work out grams per metre:** (full weight − core weight) ÷ full length.
4. **Weigh the part roll,** subtract the core, and divide by grams per metre.

### A worked example

Say a full 100 m roll weighs 420 g and an empty core weighs 30 g. That's 390 g of tape, or 3.9 g per metre. A part roll of the same tape weighs 210 g. Take off the core and you've got 180 g of tape, which is 180 ÷ 3.9 = about 46 m.

Those weights are only an example. At these weights, a couple of grams either way is half a metre of tape, so a cheap kitchen scale won't cut it.

## Which method should your shop use?

| | Diameter | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Kit needed | Steel rule you already own | Accurate scale reading to the gram |
| Cost to start | Nothing | A decent scale |
| Set-up | Optional: calibrate thickness per tape type | Weigh a full roll and a core for every tape type first |
| Start | Today | Once the scale and reference weights are sorted |

For most shops, the diameter method wins: no outlay, and you can measure every part roll on the rack this afternoon. Add weighing later if you want a cross-check on thin tape.

Whichever you use, do it every time. Update the length whenever a roll comes off the edgebander, write it on the label, and record it where the team can check before ordering.

## How much tape does the job actually need?

Now check what the job needs. Add up the banded edge length from your cutting list or cabinet software, then add:

- **End overhang** for every edge, based on your own machine settings.
- **A set-up allowance** for each colour change and test piece.
- **A small buffer** for a damaged edge or a re-run.

If the roll's length covers that total with room to spare, run it. If it's close, use the part roll on shorter parts and open a fresh one for the long runs. ## FAQ

**What's the formula for tape left on a roll?**
Length = π × (outer diameter² − core diameter²) ÷ (4 × tape thickness). Keep all measurements in millimetres and divide the answer by 1,000 to get metres.

**Should we measure or weigh our rolls?**
Start by measuring. All it takes is a steel rule you already own, so you can begin today. Weighing is a handy cross-check on thin tape if you have an accurate scale.

**Why doesn't my calculation match the length on the label?**
Real rolls aren't wound perfectly tight, and pre-glued tape is thicker than its nominal size. Calibrate using a full roll of known length to find the effective thickness.

**How often should we re-measure part rolls?**
Every time a roll comes off the edgebander and goes back on the rack. That's the moment the length changes, so that's when the record should change too.
