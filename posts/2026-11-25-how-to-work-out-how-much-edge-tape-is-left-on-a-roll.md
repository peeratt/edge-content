To work out how much edge tape is left on a roll, measure the outside diameter of the roll and the core, then use the formula: length = π × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × tape thickness). Or, for better accuracy, weigh the roll and compare it with the weight of a full roll. Either way takes about a minute and beats eyeballing it, which is how jobs end up running short halfway through.

## Why not just eyeball it?

Because the eye lies, and it lies in a predictable direction. Tape on the outside of a roll is worth a lot more than tape near the core. A roll that looks "about half full" by thickness is nowhere near half its length, because each outer wrap is far longer than an inner one.

Guess high and the roll runs out mid-job, with the edgebander stopped while someone hunts the rack for the same colour. Guess low and you order a roll you didn't need.

## Method 1: measure the diameter

All you need is a steel rule or a set of callipers.

1. **Measure the outside diameter** of the tape across the middle of the roll.
2. **Measure the core diameter** (the outside of the cardboard or plastic core, where the tape starts).
3. **Note the tape thickness**, such as 0.4 mm, 1 mm or 2 mm.
4. **Plug it in:** length (mm) = 3.1416 × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × thickness).
5. **Divide by 1,000** to get metres.

### A worked example

Say you've got a part roll of 1 mm tape. It measures 250 mm across and the core is 80 mm.

| Step | Figure |
|---|---|
| Outer diameter squared (250²) | 62,500 |
| Core diameter squared (80²) | 6,400 |
| Difference | 56,100 |
| × 3.1416 ÷ (4 × 1 mm) | about 44,060 mm |
| **Tape left on the roll** | **about 44 m** |

Now do the same roll at 400 mm across and you get about 120 m. That's the point: the roll only got 150 mm "thicker", but it holds nearly three times the tape.

### The catch: thickness isn't always what the label says

Tape is never wound perfectly tight, and pre-glued tape carries a glue layer on top of its nominal thickness. Both make the real "thickness per wrap" a bit bigger than the label, so the formula can overestimate.

The fix is to calibrate once per tape type. Take a new roll with a known length, measure its diameters, and work the formula backwards: effective thickness = 3.1416 × (outer² − core²) ÷ (4 × known length in mm). Write that figure down and use it for every part roll of the same type.

## Method 2: weigh the roll

Weighing is often more accurate, particularly for thin tape where a millimetre of error on the diameter makes a big difference. You'll need a scale that reads in grams and handles a full roll.

1. **Weigh a full roll** of a known length.
2. **Weigh an empty core** of the same type.
3. **Work out grams per metre:** (full weight − core weight) ÷ full length.
4. **Weigh the part roll,** subtract the core, and divide by grams per metre.

### A worked example

Say a full 100 m roll weighs 4.2 kg and an empty core weighs 0.3 kg. That's 3.9 kg of tape, or 39 g per metre. A part roll of the same tape weighs 2.1 kg. Take off the core and you've got 1.8 kg of tape, which is 1,800 ÷ 39 = about 46 m.

Those weights are only an example. Weigh your own tape types and pin a reference table near the scale.

## Which method should your shop use?

| | Diameter | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Kit needed | Rule or callipers | Scale reading in grams |
| Set-up | Calibrate thickness once per tape type | Weigh a full roll and a core once per tape type |
| Best for | Thick tape (1–2 mm) | Thin tape (0.4 mm) and veneer |
| Speed | About a minute | Under a minute |

The method matters less than doing it every time. Update the length whenever a roll comes off the edgebander, write it on the label, and record it where the team can check before ordering.

## How much tape does the job actually need?

Knowing what's on the roll only helps if you know what the job needs. Add up the banded edge length from your cutting list or cabinet software, then add:

- **End overhang** for every edge, based on your own machine settings.
- **A set-up allowance** for each colour change and test piece.
- **A small buffer** for a damaged edge or a re-run.

If the roll's length covers that total with room to spare, run it. If it's close, use the part roll on shorter parts and open a fresh one for the long runs. ## FAQ

**What's the formula for tape left on a roll?**
Length = π × (outer diameter² − core diameter²) ÷ (4 × tape thickness). Keep all measurements in millimetres and divide the answer by 1,000 to get metres.

**Is weighing a roll more accurate than measuring it?**
Usually, especially on thin tape. You need a decent scale and the weight of a full roll and an empty core for each tape type, but after that it's quick and repeatable.

**Why doesn't my calculation match the length on the label?**
Real rolls aren't wound perfectly tight, and pre-glued tape is thicker than its nominal size. Calibrate using a full roll of known length to find the effective thickness.

**How often should we re-measure part rolls?**
Every time a roll comes off the edgebander and goes back on the rack. That's the moment the length changes, so that's when the record should change too.
