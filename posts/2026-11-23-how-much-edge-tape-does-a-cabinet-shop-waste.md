Most cabinet shops waste far more edge tape through poor stock control than through the edgebander. The trim off each panel is small and predictable; the expensive waste is reordering tape you already own, running short mid-job because a roll was guessed, and losing part rolls on the rack. If you want to know your real number, count the duplicate orders and lost hours, not just the offcuts in the bin.

## Where does edge tape actually go?

Every metre of tape that comes through the door ends up in one of a few places. Some of it is on finished cabinets, which is the bit you get paid for. The rest disappears into:

- **Machine overhang and trim.** Each edge is fed with a bit of extra tape at both ends so the end trim unit has something to cut. Small per edge, but it adds up across a job.
- **Start-up and test pieces.** Setting up a new colour, checking glue and adjusting the trimmers all eat tape.
- **Roll ends.** The last few metres on a roll often aren't enough for a long panel, so they sit on the rack or go in the bin.
- **Damaged stock.** Rolls stored badly get dented edges, dust in the glue line or a crushed core.
- **Duplicate stock.** Rolls you bought again because nobody could find, or trust, the one you already had.

The first three are a cost of doing business. The last two are pure waste, and they're usually the biggest.

## How much does the edgebander itself waste?

It depends on your machine, your settings and your parts, so work it out for your own shop rather than trusting a rule of thumb. Here's a worked example to show the method.

Say a shop sets roughly 30 mm of overhang at each end of every edge (check your own machine's manual and settings for your actual figure). A job of 60 panels with an average of three banded edges each is 180 edges.

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Edges run | 180 |
| Overhang per edge (both ends) | 60 mm |
| Tape lost to end trim | 10.8 m |

On a 100 m roll, that's about a tenth of a roll per job. Add a metre or two for set-up on each colour change and you've got a fair picture.

## What does a duplicate roll cost?

When stock isn't labelled and recorded, the shop doesn't really know what it has. So when a job comes in calling for a particular colour, someone has a quick look, can't see it, and orders another roll.

That one decision costs you three ways:

1. **The roll itself.** A full roll you didn't need, now sitting next to the one you already had.
2. **The delivery.** A small urgent order often carries its own freight charge, and sometimes a minimum order fee.
3. **The time.** Someone spent twenty minutes looking before giving up, and the job may have waited for the new roll to arrive.

One duplicate roll can easily wipe out more than a whole job's worth of end trim. And unlike trim, it doesn't show up in the bin. It shows up quietly on the supplier invoice.

## Why do guessed roll lengths cause so much waste?

Most shops eyeball a part roll and guess how much is left. Guess high, and the roll runs out halfway down a long run of panels. Now you're stopping the edgebander, hunting for another roll of the same colour, and possibly re-running parts. Guess low, and you order a new roll you didn't need.

Either way, the guess costs money. Measuring the roll gives you an actual length, so you can decide with confidence whether it'll cover the job.

## How do you measure your own edge tape waste?

You don't need a consultant. Spend a month tracking these four things:

1. **Tape purchased.** Pull the supplier invoices and total the metres by colour.
2. **Tape on finished jobs.** Your cabinet software or cutting lists should give you banded length per job. Add your overhang allowance.
3. **Duplicate or urgent orders.** Count every order placed for a colour you later found on the rack.
4. **Search time.** Ask the team to jot down any time they spend looking for a roll.

The gap between what you bought and what went on cabinets is your real waste figure. If the duplicate orders and search time are bigger than the machine trim, and in most shops they will be, you know where to start.

## From the workshop

We see the same pattern over and over: shops guess how much is on a roll, reorder to be safe, then find the roll they already had sitting on the rack. We call it the triple loss: you pay for the duplicate tape, you pay the extra delivery cost, and you pay for the time spent looking for the roll in the first place. None of that is a machine problem. It's an information problem, and the cheapest kind to fix.

## What are the quickest fixes?

- **Label every roll** with its colour, width, thickness and a code you can scan.
- **Record the length** when a roll comes in and update it when it goes back on the rack.
- **Give every roll a home** and write the rack location into your records.
- **Check before you order.** Make "look it up first" a rule, not a suggestion.
- **Use up part rolls** on shorter parts before opening a fresh one.

## FAQ

**What's the biggest source of edge tape waste in a cabinet shop?**
Usually not machine trim. It's buying tape you already own because nobody can find it or knows how much is left on the roll.

**How much tape does the edgebander itself waste?**
Each edge needs a little overhang at both ends for end trimming. Check your machine settings, multiply by the number of edges run, and you'll have your real figure.

**How do I stop reordering tape we already have?**
Label every roll, record its current length and where it's stored, and check that record before you place an order.

**Is it worth tracking short rolls and part rolls?**
Yes. Part rolls are where most hidden stock sits. If they're not recorded, they get forgotten and replaced.
