For most cabinet shops, ABS is the sensible default plastic edge tape for visible edges, melamine is fine for low-wear and hidden edges, and PVC earns its spot only if you already run it well and your clients aren't asking for chlorine-free materials. The bigger win is choosing one main plastic, not two. Every material you add multiplies the rolls sitting on your rack.

## What's actually different between ABS, PVC and melamine?

All three do the same job: cover the raw edge of a board and make it look like it belongs there. They get there in different ways.

- **ABS** is a thermoplastic with no chlorine in it. It trims and scrapes cleanly, comes in thick profiles (1 mm, 2 mm and up), and is generally easier to recycle. It can get a bit brittle in a cold workshop, so let rolls warm up before running them in winter.
- **PVC** is the old workhorse. It's tough, flexible and forgiving on tight radii, and plenty of shops have run it for decades without a complaint. The knocks against it are the chlorine content and what happens to it at the end of its life.
- **Melamine** is resin-impregnated paper, usually thin (around 0.3–0.5 mm) and often pre-glued. It's cheap and quick to apply, but it chips, it won't take a decent radius, and it shows wear fast.

## How do they compare on the job?

Here's a rough comparison from the bench. Your supplier's data sheet beats this table every time, so check it for specific products.

| | ABS | PVC | Melamine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impact resistance | Good | Very good | Poor |
| Typical thickness | 0.4–3 mm | 0.4–3 mm | 0.3–0.5 mm |
| Takes a radius | Yes | Yes | No |
| Chlorine-free | Yes | No | Yes |
| Cold-weather fussiness | Some | Less | Some (glue) |
| Best for | Most visible edges | Heavy-use edges, if already set up | Hidden and budget edges |

## Which edges get which tape?

Go by where the edge lives and what's going to hit it.

1. **Doors, drawer fronts, kickboards, benchtop edges:** plastic, 1 mm or thicker. These take vacuum cleaners, shoes and shopping bags. ABS or PVC, your call, but pick one.
2. **Visible carcass edges and open shelving:** plastic, usually 0.4–1 mm, matched to the board.
3. **Hidden edges, backs of shelves, edges behind doors:** melamine is fine. Nobody's going to run a finger along the back edge of an internal shelf.
4. **Budget jobs and quick turnaround work:** melamine where the client has accepted the trade-off. Put it on the quote so there's no argument later.

## Should you stock both ABS and PVC?

Probably not. This is where shops quietly lose money.

Say you run 30 board colours that need plastic edging, and you stock each in two thicknesses. That's 60 roll types. Add both ABS and PVC and you're at 120, and that's before part rolls. Your money is now sitting on the rack, and the chance of grabbing the wrong roll has doubled.

There are a few good reasons to stock both:

- A big client specifies one material and you can't talk them out of it.
- You do a lot of tight-radius work and you've found PVC easier on your setup.
- You're mid-changeover and running down old stock.

If none of those apply, pick one plastic and commit. The edgebander settings get simpler, staff stop second-guessing, and the reorder list gets shorter.

## Does switching materials mean changing machine settings?

Sometimes. Different materials can want different glue temperatures, feed speeds and trimming setups. Plastic tape that's been sitting in a cold shed can also give you poor adhesion until it's back to room temperature.

Don't guess. Read the tape and glue suppliers' technical data sheets and your edgebander's manual, and run a few test pieces before a full job. If anything involves guarding or safety settings, go by the manufacturer's manual and SafeWork guidance, not a blog post.

## From the workshop

One thing we see over and over: the waste isn't just offcuts on the floor. It's ordering edge tape you already have, because the stock on hand isn't labelled and nobody can say for sure what's there.

Carry two plastics and that problem gets worse. A white 1 mm roll of ABS and a white 1 mm roll of PVC look almost identical on a rack. If the label's missing, someone either uses the wrong one or gives up and reorders. And every small top-up order tends to bring its own delivery charge.

So whatever mix you settle on, label every roll with material, colour, thickness and what's left on it. It's the cheapest way to stop paying twice for the same tape.

## A simple way to decide this week

1. List every edge tape you hold, split by material.
2. Mark which ones you've actually used in the last three months.
3. For each colour stocked in both ABS and PVC, pick one to keep and run the other down.
4. Move melamine to hidden-edge and budget work only.
5. Label what's left so the next order is based on what's really there.

That's an afternoon's work, and it usually frees up rack space and cash.

## FAQ

**Is ABS or PVC edge tape better?**
Neither wins on every job. ABS is chlorine-free and trims cleanly, so it's the safer default for most visible edges. PVC is tough and flexible, and fine if you already run it well. Pick one as your main plastic so the rack doesn't double up.

**When is melamine edge tape good enough?**
On edges that don't take knocks: shelf backs, carcass edges hidden by doors, and budget work. It's thin and brittle, so keep it off kickboards, drawer fronts and benchtop edges.

**Can I run ABS and PVC through the same edgebander?**
Usually, yes, but they can behave differently on glue temperature, feed speed and trimming. Check the tape and glue suppliers' data sheets and your machine manual before changing settings.

**How many edge tape materials should a small shop stock?**
Most small shops do fine with one plastic (ABS or PVC) for visible edges plus melamine for hidden or budget work. Every extra material multiplies the number of rolls you have to track.
